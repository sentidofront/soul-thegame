SOUL · design proposal

Spawn Director

The spawner currently keeps a headcount. Vampire Survivors runs a schedule. That single difference is why our crowds feel like weather and theirs feel like an author is in the room — and it is fixable without touching the bestiary.

01

What ours does now

Every frame, updateSpawner computes a target population and tops it up:

StepBehaviour
RecycleAnything past DESPAWN_RADIUS (780) is deleted
TargetbaseDensity + densityRamp × (distance/1000), plus a small time term
FillSpend spawnCredit (24/sec) until the population reaches target
Place82% inside a cone centred on the aim, at the view edge; 18% anywhere
WavesEvery 42s, a full ring of 32→110 bodies at once

It works, and it has four structural problems:

  • Density is a scalar, so the fight has no shape. A number of bodies arrives per second from roughly the same place, for ever. Nothing about minute six is different in kind from minute two — only bigger.
  • Composition is a weight table, so species arrive blended. Once four things are unlocked you get a smoothie of all four rather than "here come the saleiros".
  • The one wave type is the only punctuation we have — and it is always the same full ring.
  • The kill-count gates are coupled to enemy health. Already re-derived twice this project, for exactly that reason.
02

What Vampire Survivors actually does

Researched rather than assumed. The load-bearing facts:

MechanicHow it works there
Waves are the unit One wave per minute of stage time. The wave — not a density curve — is what defines who shows up and how fast.
Quota, then top-up Each wave names a minimum population and a spawn interval. Below quota, it spawns until the quota is filled. At or above quota, it spawns one of each type in the wave per interval.
Hard population cap At 300 alive, periodic spawning stops entirely. Only bosses and map events can still spawn.
Off-screen spawn, distance despawn Enemies appear just outside the view and are recycled when the player gets far enough away. Bosses do not despawn — they teleport back into view.
Map events Scheduled set pieces outside the normal cycle: a swarm sweeping across the screen, or a ring of high-health enemies closing on the player. Tied to a wave, firing at the same second every run.
Cycles Past the last minute, waves restart from minute 0 and the stage counts as complete — the table loops rather than extrapolating.

The insight worth stealing is the second row. Their spawner is not a rate — it is a quota with a composition. "Keep forty of these on the field, and every two seconds add one of each kind in this wave" produces a fight with a recognisable character, and it is barely more code than what we have.

Worth noting what they don't do: there is no aim-cone weighting, no distance-driven density, and no continuous difficulty curve. Escalation is entirely authored, one table row per minute.

03

The proposal

Replace the density scalar with a wave table per act, and add a small library of formations so that arrivals have shape. Keep our two genuinely good ideas — the facing cone, and progression driven by distance rather than a clock.

The wave table

Each act becomes a list of entries keyed to distance, not time — this is a journey, and that difference is ours to keep:

FieldMeaning
fromXWorld X this entry takes over at
quotaPopulation to hold on the field
intervalSeconds between top-up spawns once at quota
castWhich species, and in what ratio — the wave's character
event?An optional formation to fire on entry

This kills the kill-count gates outright. A species appears because a wave asks for it, so the bestiary's pacing stops being an emergent consequence of how tanky things happen to be — which is what has broken it twice.

Formations

Right now everything arrives one way: scattered at the view edge, mostly ahead. Four shapes cover almost everything VS does with its map events, and each says something different:

Two rules to import wholesale

  • A hard cap that stops the trickle, not the events. Ours is MAX_ENEMIES: 700 and applies to everything. Theirs is 300 and exempts bosses and events — so a set piece always lands, even in a crowd. That exemption is the good part.
  • Elites and bosses never despawn. Ours already skips elites in the recycler. VS goes further and teleports them back into view. Worth copying for the Doido do Carro, which currently vanishes if you outrun it.
04

How I would build it

Ordered so that each step is shippable on its own and testable in the console.

01

Quota replaces density

small

Swap baseDensity/densityRamp for quota and interval per wave entry, with the top-up rule: below quota, fill; at quota, one of each species in the cast per interval. No new content — the existing tables become one wave entry each, so behaviour is near-identical and everything downstream keeps working.

02

The wave table, keyed to distance

medium

Write 6–8 entries per act with explicit casts. Delete unlockKills entirely — a species now appears because a wave asks for it. This is where the bestiary's pacing stops being an accident of enemy health, and it is the step that pays for the whole rework.

03

Formations

small

One function, four shapes: cone, ring, wall, pincer. The current wave code already does ring — this generalises it and lets a wave entry name one.

04

Set pieces

medium

Scheduled, authored moments: the cattle stampede on the road, a glowie cordon across a Floriano street, the car doing a run down the highway with a wall behind it. These are what people will remember, and none of them are possible while spawning is a scalar.

05

Cap and despawn rules

small

Cap exempts bosses and set pieces. Elites teleport back into view instead of vanishing. Re-measure the frame cost at the new cap.

05

Calls I need from you

Distance or time?

VS keys everything to stage minutes. Ours is a journey with a destination, so I would key waves to world X and keep time as a minor term — but that means a player who stands still stops progressing the bestiary, which VS never has to think about. My recommendation: distance.

Does the population cap come down?

Ours is 700, theirs is 300. We measured 415 on screen at 1.08 ms a frame, so 700 is affordable — but "affordable" and "readable" are different questions, and at current contact damage a crowd of 700 is not a fight anyone survives. My recommendation: 300, with events exempt.

Do the aim-cone spawns survive?

It is ours, not VS's, and it is genuinely good — pointing somewhere means walking into more of it. But it fights formations: a ring is not a ring if 82% of it arrives in front of you. My recommendation: cone stays for the trickle, formations ignore it.

How much of the bestiary's pacing do you want authored?

The kill gates were an attempt to have pacing emerge on its own. It has not worked twice. Authoring it per wave is more writing but it is the only version that stays correct when you retune enemy health — which you do regularly, and should be able to keep doing.
